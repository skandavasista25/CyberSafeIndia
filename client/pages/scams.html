Latest Online Scams in India

Tap any card to see how each scam works, the warning signs, and how to protect yourself.

UPI Scams

Overview

Fraudsters exploit UPI's speed and simplicity to trick people into approving payments that move money out of their account, disguised as requests to receive money.

How it works

The scammer sends a UPI "collect request" or asks the victim to enter their UPI PIN, claiming it's needed to "receive" a refund, prize, or payment. Entering the PIN actually authorises an outgoing payment.

Warning signs

  • You're asked to enter a UPI PIN to "receive" money — receiving never needs a PIN.
  • Urgency: "approve now or the offer expires."
  • A stranger claiming to be a buyer on an online marketplace.

Prevention tips

  • Remember: a UPI PIN is only ever needed to send money, never to receive it.
  • Decline unexpected collect requests instead of approving on autopilot.
  • Set a low daily UPI transaction limit in your banking app.

OTP Scams

Overview

An OTP (one-time password) is the last lock on your account. Scammers don't try to break it — they simply ask you to hand over the key.

How it works

A caller poses as a bank employee, delivery agent, or "verification team" and asks you to read out the OTP you just received, claiming it's needed to "cancel", "verify", or "process" something.

Warning signs

  • Any call or message asking you to share an OTP out loud or by text.
  • Pressure to act within minutes "or your card will be blocked".
  • An OTP arrives when you did not initiate any transaction.

Prevention tips

  • No genuine bank, company, or government office will ever ask for your OTP.
  • Never share an OTP over call, SMS, WhatsApp, or email — full stop.
  • If an unexpected OTP arrives, treat it as a signal someone is trying to log in — change your password right away.

QR Code Scams

Overview

QR codes feel harmless because they're everywhere — but scanning one to "receive" a payment is exactly how the UPI-PIN trick is delivered.

How it works

A "buyer" sends a QR code and asks the victim to scan it and enter their PIN to "get" a payment. Some scammers also paste fake QR stickers over real ones at shops and parking stands, redirecting payment to themselves.

Warning signs

  • Being told to scan a code and enter a PIN in order to receive funds.
  • A QR sticker that looks tampered with, crooked, or pasted over another one.
  • The payee name shown after scanning doesn't match the shop or person you expect.

Prevention tips

  • Scanning a QR code is only for paying someone — never for receiving money.
  • Always check the payee name displayed before confirming a payment.
  • At shops, check the QR sticker looks original and matches the merchant's name.

Fake Job Scams

Overview

Fake "work from home" and part-time job offers target job seekers, especially students and freshers, promising easy money for simple tasks.

How it works

Recruiters contact victims over WhatsApp or Telegram with offers for tasks like "liking videos" or "rating products," and ask for a small "registration" or "activation" fee upfront — which grows before any payout ever arrives.

Warning signs

  • Job offers that arrive unsolicited over chat apps rather than official portals.
  • Any request to pay a fee before you can start earning.
  • Unusually high pay for extremely simple tasks.

Prevention tips

  • A genuine employer never asks you to pay to get hired.
  • Verify the company's name, website, and reviews independently before engaging.
  • Be wary of interviews conducted entirely over chat with no verifiable company details.

Investment Scams

Overview

Fake trading platforms and "investment advisors" promise guaranteed high returns on stocks, crypto, or forex to pull in savings that are never returned.

How it works

Victims are added to social media or messaging groups showing fabricated profit screenshots. Early "withdrawals" are allowed to build trust, then larger deposits are requested before withdrawals are suddenly blocked.

Warning signs

  • "Guaranteed" or fixed daily/weekly returns — real markets never promise this.
  • Pressure to recruit friends or deposit more to "unlock" a withdrawal.
  • An app or platform with no SEBI/RBI registration you can verify.

Prevention tips

  • Only invest through SEBI-registered brokers and platforms.
  • Treat "guaranteed returns" claims as an automatic red flag.
  • Never invest based on tips from unknown social media or WhatsApp groups.

Fake Customer Care Scams

Overview

Scammers publish fake "customer care" numbers for banks, e-commerce sites, and airlines that rank highly on search engines and social media.

How it works

A victim searching for a support number calls the fake listing. The "agent" asks for remote-access app installs, card details, or OTPs to "resolve" a complaint, and instead drains the account.

Warning signs

  • A support number found via a general web search rather than the official app or website.
  • Being asked to install a remote screen-sharing app to "fix" an issue.
  • Requests for card numbers, CVV, or OTP to "process a refund".

Prevention tips

  • Get support numbers only from the official app, website footer, or your bank passbook/statement.
  • Never install remote-access apps for someone who called or messaged you first.
  • Refunds never require you to share OTPs, PINs, or card CVVs.

Delivery Scams

Overview

Fake courier and customs messages exploit the fact that almost everyone is expecting a parcel at any given time.

How it works

An SMS or WhatsApp message claims a parcel is "held at customs" or "couldn't be delivered" and asks the victim to click a link and pay a small fee, which harvests card details on a fake payment page.

Warning signs

  • A delivery fee requested through a link rather than the courier's own app.
  • A tracking link that doesn't match the courier company's official domain.
  • A parcel you don't recall ordering.

Prevention tips

  • Track parcels only through the official courier app or website, typed in manually.
  • Be suspicious of any "customs fee" requested by SMS link — legitimate customs dues aren't collected this way.
  • Hover over or long-press links to preview the real destination before tapping.

Already spotted one of these? Move fast.

If you've shared an OTP, PIN, or made a payment by mistake, report it immediately on the national helpline.
