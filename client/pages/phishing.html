How to Identify Phishing

Phishing tries to make a fake message feel official. Here's what each version looks like, and the tells that give it away.

Email Phishing

Emails pretending to be from a bank, employer, or well-known company, designed to make you click a link or open an attachment.

  • Sender address looks close to real but slightly off (e.g. support@sbi-secure.info).
  • Generic greeting like "Dear Customer" instead of your name.
  • Urgent subject lines: "Account Suspended", "Action Required in 24 Hours".
Example — do not click

From: accounts@sbi-secure.info
Subject: Your account will be suspended today

Dear Customer,
We noticed unusual activity. Click here within 24 hours to verify your KYC or your account will be permanently blocked.

SMS Phishing (Smishing)

Short, urgent text messages with a link — designed to be read quickly on a phone, where fake URLs are harder to inspect.

  • Shortened or unfamiliar links instead of the bank's real domain.
  • Claims about KYC expiry, blocked cards, or electricity disconnection.
  • Sent from a random 10-digit number rather than an official sender ID.
Example — do not click

Dear User, your KYC will expire today. Update immediately: bit.ly/kyc-updt3 to avoid account block. — Bank Team

Voice Phishing (Vishing)

A phone call from someone claiming to be a bank officer, police official, or government representative, built to sound convincing in real time.

  • Caller already knows some of your details, which feels reassuring but is often gathered elsewhere.
  • Requests to install a "verification" or remote-access app during the call.
  • Threats — blocked SIM, frozen account, an "arrest warrant" — designed to stop you thinking clearly.
Example call script

"This is [Bank Name] Security Department. Your card was used in Mumbai just now. To block this transaction, please confirm the OTP sent to your phone."

Fake Login Pages

Near-identical copies of a bank or email login page, hosted on a look-alike domain, built purely to capture your username and password.

  • The web address doesn't exactly match the real domain (extra words, hyphens, or a different extension like .info).
  • No padlock/HTTPS, or a certificate warning in the browser.
  • The page asks for extra fields a real login never asks for together, like PIN + OTP + CVV at once.
Example address

Real: www.onlinesbi.sbi
Fake: www.online-sbi-login.info/verify

Suspicious Links

The link itself is often the biggest clue, no matter which channel it arrives through.

  • Shortened links (bit.ly, tinyurl) that hide the true destination.
  • Misspelled brand names or extra characters (amaz0n, faceb00k-login).
  • Unusual domain endings for a well-known brand (.xyz, .top, .info).
How to check

On desktop, hover over a link to preview the real URL in the status bar. On mobile, long-press the link to see a preview before opening it. When in doubt, type the site's known address directly into your browser instead.

Passwords are the next layer of defence.

Even if a phishing attempt slips through, a strong, unique password and 2FA can stop it from turning into a takeover.
