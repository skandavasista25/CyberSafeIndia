Safe UPI Practices

UPI is fast and convenient — a few simple habits keep it that way without opening the door to fraud.

Never share your OTP or UPI PIN

Your UPI PIN and any OTP linked to it are meant for you alone to enter. No bank, UPI app, or "verification team" will ever call or message asking you to read one out. If someone asks, the conversation is the scam.

Verify payment requests before approving

A "collect request" notification means someone is asking to pull money from your account — approving it authorises an outgoing payment. Check the requester's name and reason carefully, and when in doubt, decline and confirm with the person directly through another channel.

Avoid unknown QR codes

Only scan a QR code when you intend to pay someone. Scanning a code and entering your PIN is never how you receive money. Be cautious of QR stickers at shops that look pasted-over, crooked, or mismatched with the merchant's name.

Keep online banking secure

Use your bank's official app downloaded from the official app store, keep it updated, set a strong app-lock PIN or biometric lock, and avoid logging into net banking on shared or public computers.

Recognise the pattern

Common UPI fraud techniques

Most UPI frauds follow one of these patterns — recognising the shape of the trick is often enough to avoid it.

Fake "refund" collect requests

A scammer poses as an online seller processing a refund, but sends a collect request instead of an actual payment.

Marketplace buyer scams

A "buyer" on OLX or a similar app insists on paying via a link or QR code that actually requests money from you.

Look-alike UPI apps

Fake apps mimicking popular UPI apps are sideloaded outside app stores to capture PINs and banking details.

Remote-access app tricks

Victims are talked into installing a screen-sharing app "for support," letting scammers watch PIN entry in real time.

SIM-swap fraud

Fraudsters obtain a duplicate SIM through social engineering to intercept OTPs and take over UPI and banking apps.

Public Wi-Fi interception

Making payments over unsecured public Wi-Fi increases the risk of traffic interception on poorly secured networks.

Know exactly what to do if something goes wrong.

Fast reporting is the single biggest factor in recovering money lost to UPI fraud.
