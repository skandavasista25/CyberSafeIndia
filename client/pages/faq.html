Frequently Asked Questions

The questions people ask most often about staying safe online in India.

Call the 1930 cyber crime helpline immediately, then contact your bank to block your card or freeze your account. File a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in as soon as possible — speed matters most in the first hour.

Scanning a QR code just opens a payment screen — it can't withdraw money by itself. The danger is entering your UPI PIN afterward, which authorises an outgoing payment. Never enter your PIN to "receive" money.

Only save card details on well-known, reputable sites using tokenisation (where your actual card number isn't stored). Avoid saving details on unfamiliar or unverified websites.

Genuine bank messages usually come from a short alphanumeric sender ID (like "SBIBNK"), never ask for your PIN or OTP, and don't include shortened links. When unsure, open your bank's app directly instead of clicking any link in the message.

Yes — the real risk isn't one weak password, it's reusing even a strong one across multiple sites. A password manager lets you use a unique password everywhere without needing to remember each one.

Public Wi-Fi is fine for casual browsing, but avoid banking, shopping, or logging into sensitive accounts on it. Use mobile data or a trusted VPN for anything involving money or personal information.

Never share your OTP, UPI PIN, CVV, net banking password, or card expiry date with anyone over call, SMS, or chat — no genuine bank or company representative needs these to help you.

Check the company's official website and reviews independently, confirm the offer through the company's verified email domain, and remember that legitimate employers never ask candidates to pay a fee to get hired.
